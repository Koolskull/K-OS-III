# 01 — The way out

> **Track:** Creativity as the Way Out · **Lesson 1 of 6**
> **Tools shipped with this lesson:** `tools/state-inventory.md` (see also `tools/first-build-template.md`)

---

## What this lesson is for

Depression, chronic laziness, imposter syndrome, and hopelessness are not primarily problems of willpower or character. They are states that follow from experiences that have neurologically and permanently reshaped a person's expectations of the world. This lesson does not ask you to argue with that reshaping. It offers a different move: **build something**.

The reasoning is that creative production is one of the few activities that simultaneously:

- produces external, verifiable evidence of agency,
- generates value that others can recognize, and
- does not require you to first feel worthy or motivated.

The output precedes the feeling. This is the tract's answer to imposter syndrome: the impostor feeling is about identity, and identity is downstream of what you have made.

---

## The state inventory

Before you build, name where you are. Fill in the three lines in `tools/state-inventory.md`:

1. **The state** — which one is loudest right now: depression, laziness, imposter syndrome, hopelessness, or something else?
2. **When it started** — not a diagnosis, just a date or a season. When did this become the default?
3. **What you built last week** — anything. A meal. A message. A line of code. If the honest answer is "nothing," that is the data.

The gap between line two and line three is the opening. Not a verdict. An opening.

---

## Why this works when nothing else does

Most advice aimed at these states asks the person to change how they feel first, then act. That order fails for a trauma-shaped nervous system, because the feeling is the symptom of the reshaping — it will not lead. Creative production reverses the order: act first, at the smallest scale that is real, and let the feeling follow the evidence.

"Real" here means the same thing it means in section 3.2 of the source tract: if it is real, it has value; if it is a facade, it will not maintain long-term value, because there is nothing inside it. A first build does not need to be good. It needs to be *there* — something a person other than you could point at.
