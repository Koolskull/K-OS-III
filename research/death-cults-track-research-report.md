## 3. Section 1: Mexica (Aztec) child sacrifice

### Claim 1.1: The Mexica sacrificed children to the rain god Tlaloc.

**Primary sources**

- **Archaeology: INAH Boletín 696** (11 Nov 2024), "Ofrenda masiva de infantes dedicada a Tláloc fue coincidente con la gran sequía de 1454 en la Cuenca de México" (https://inah.gob.mx/boletines/ofrenda-masiva-de-infantes-dedicada-a-tlaloc-fue-coincidente-con-la-gran-sequia-de-1454-en-la-cuenca-de-mexico). VERIFIED. Offering 48 at the Templo Mayor:
  - Excavated by the Templo Mayor Project between late July 1980 and early January 1981.
  - **At least 42 children**, aged about 2–7. Sex was determined for 28 of them: 22 male, 6 female.
  - Physical anthropologist Juan Alberto Román Berrelleza found porotic hyperostosis (a marker of illness or malnutrition) in about half.
  - Buried in a stone box (170 × 111 × 54 cm) with 11 Tláloc jars.
  - Construction stage IVa (Moctezuma Ilhuicamina).
  - Now tied to the drought of 1452–1454 (year 1 Rabbit = 1454).
- **Isotopes:** the bulletin reports Diana Moreiras Reynaga's (UBC) phosphate-oxygen isotope results. The children came largely from outside the Basin, with Oaxaca indicated and one individual from highland Chiapas/Guatemala. Paper: Moreiras Reynaga et al. 2021, *Journal of Anthropological Archaeology* (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278416521000295). Abstract seen via search only, UNVERIFIED.
- **Tlatelolco:** a separate child offering at the Ehécatl-Quetzalcóatl temple (43 children reported by *Proceso*, 2018; UNVERIFIED journalism, used only to locate the find). Overview: Román Berrelleza & Torre Blanco, "Sacrificios de niños en el Templo Mayor," *Arqueología Mexicana* (https://arqueologiamexicana.mx/mexico-antiguo/sacrificios-de-ninos-en-el-templo-mayor). UNVERIFIED (snippet only).
- **Text: Florentine Codex (Sahagún), Book 2, ch. 1.** The Spanish text for the month Atlcahualo: "En este mes mataban muchos niños; sacrificábanlos en muchos lugares, en las cumbres de los montes, sacándoles los corazones a honra de los dioses del agua, para que les diesen agua o lluvia." The Spanish wording is from an excerpt reproduction. The Getty Digital Florentine Codex (https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/) exists, but its pages render by JavaScript and I could not extract the text. Treat the exact wording as UNVERIFIED-against-manuscript.
- **Text: Florentine Codex, Book 2, ch. 20 (Nahuatl column).** Kruell, "El texto náhuatl del capítulo 20, libro II, del Códice Florentino," *Estudios de Cultura Náhuatl* 64 (2022): 215–252 (https://nahuatl.historicas.unam.mx/index.php/ecn/article/download/78109/69109). VERIFIED. Kruell's Spanish translation:
  > "Y allá iban a dejar también a unos niñitos, llamados tlacateteuhtin (tiras humanas). Ellos tenían dos remolinos en la cabeza y su signo calendárico era bueno. En todas partes eran buscados, eran comprados."
  Named sites include Tepetzinco and Pantitlan. **Children were sought out and purchased.** That is a primary statement about procurement, not about a secret society.

**Steelman (for the claim).** Text and archaeology converge. Sahagún's Nahua informants describe child sacrifice to the water deities. Children were selected by physical sign and day-sign and were bought. Excavation then produced a sealed box of at least 42 children with Tláloc iconography, dated to a historically recorded drought. Isotopes show many of the children were brought from distant regions, which fits the "eran comprados" (they were bought) of the Nahuatl text. This is as solid as pre-modern history gets.

**Counter-steelman.**

- **Sahagún's limits.** His project was a missionary one, compiled decades after the Conquest. The Spanish column is shorter and sometimes harsher than the Nahuatl; Kruell's edition exists partly because the Nahuatl column differs.
- **Peter Hassler (dissenting).** *Menschenopfer bei den Azteken?* (Peter Lang, 1992) argued that the Spanish sources are ideologically contaminated and that sacrifice was exaggerated or invented. Partially verified via catalogue record.
- **Hassler's case against Offering 48.** Hassler's position predates most of the isotope and osteology work. It cannot account for physical remains with Tláloc jars sealed under a 15th-century floor.

**Status: DOCUMENTED** (mainstream). Hassler's denial is a **dissenting** position that the physical evidence has overtaken.

### Claim 1.2: Mexica sacrifice happened at enormous scale (e.g., 80,400 victims at the 1487 rededication; 20,000 a year).

**Primary sources**

- **Chroniclers, as tabulated by Harner 1977** ("The Ecological Basis for Aztec Sacrifice," *American Ethnologist*; PDF https://www.latinamericanstudies.org/aztecs/aztec_sacrifice.pdf; VERIFIED):
  - For 1487, "the Codex Telleriano-Remensis states 20,000; Torquemada reports 72,344; Tezozomoc, Ixtlilxochitl, and Duran all give 80,400 (Cook 1946:90; Prescott 1936:49n)."
  - On the annual figure, Harner quotes Prescott: "Bishop Zumarraga... states that 20,000 victims were yearly slaughtered in the capital. Torquemada turns this into 20,000 infants." **This shows a child-sacrifice number being inflated within the colonial sources themselves.**
- **Andrés de Tapia**, quoted by Harner from the conquistador's own account: he and Gonzalo de Umbría counted the poles and "found that there were 136,000 skulls, not counting the ones on the towers."
- **Huei Tzompantli** (archaeology), found 2015 at República de Guatemala 24 by INAH's Programa de Arqueología Urbana (Raúl Barrera Rodríguez):
  - About 484 skulls plus 119 more were reported by Dec 2020 (El País / El Universal / BBC Mundo; UNVERIFIED journalism).
  - **655 skulls** per INAH Boletín 98 (2023): 60% male, 38% female, 2% infants. URL: https://www.inah.gob.mx/images/boletines/2023/98/Boletin%2098.pdf (snippet; UNVERIFIED as opened).
- **Chávez Balderas**, *The Offering of Life* (Tulane PhD dissertation, 2019): the osteological study of the West Plaza victims (UNVERIFIED).

**Steelman (for large scale).** Three independent kinds of evidence point the same way: multiple chroniclers from different traditions (Mexica-descended Tezozómoc, Texcocan Ixtlilxóchitl, Dominican Durán); conquistador eyewitnesses who counted skull racks; and archaeology that keeps adding skulls. The excavated tzompantli is only partly exposed, so 655 is a minimum, not a total. Harner and Cook (15,000/year in Central Mexico) took the scale seriously.

**Counter-steelman.**

- **The numbers disagree among themselves.** 20,000 vs 72,344 vs 80,400 for one event. Even Harner says "the significance of the figures given for victims is uncertain."
- **Logistics.** 80,400 in four days implies a killing rate that skeptics question.
- **Matthew Restall** (*When Montezuma Met Cortés*, 2018; UNVERIFIED, not opened) and **Gilbert Estrada** (Mexicolore, 29 Jul 2020, https://www.mexicolore.co.uk/aztecs/home/nearly-everything-you-were-taught-about-aztec-sacrifice-is-wrong; VERIFIED as Estrada's article) argue that Spanish numbers served to justify conquest. Estrada's specific counts ("only 126 people," "180 skulls") are **out of date** against INAH's 655. They should be attributed to him and corrected, not repeated.
- **Tapia's 136,000** is a multiplication estimate, not a count. Modern archaeologists have *not* recovered anything near that number.

**Status.** That sacrifice was large-scale and systematic: **DOCUMENTED**. Specific chronicle totals (80,400; 20,000/yr; 136,000 skulls): **CONTESTED**. Teach them as *reported figures*, attributed by source, beside the excavated minimum.

### Claim 1.3: The "Flower Wars" (xochiyaoyotl) were a ritual arrangement to supply sacrificial victims.

**Primary sources.** Isaac, Barry L. 1983, "The Aztec 'Flowery War': A Geopolitical Explanation," *Journal of Anthropological Research* (PDF https://www.latinamericanstudies.org/aztecs/aztec_warfare.pdf; VERIFIED; volume/issue believed to be 39(4), UNVERIFIED). Isaac reviews the chronicle record (Chimalpahin, Ixtlilxóchitl, Durán, and others).

**Steelman.** Texcocan sources (Ixtlilxóchitl) describe a treaty-based "war of flowers" with Tlaxcala, Huexotzinco, and Cholula, fought to train warriors and take captives for the gods. If so, the sacrificial economy shaped warfare itself.

**Counter-steelman (Isaac).** Isaac argues the ritual explanation may be a later cover story:

> "it is entirely possible that the supposed treaty origin of the intervalley Flowery War, reported only by writers from Tetzcoco and their copiers, was a post hoc rationalization disseminated by the Tetzcocan elite"

He also writes: "What empire has not put forth, and fervently believed, religious rationalizations..." On his reading, the Flowery War was ordinary geopolitical warfare against unconquered rivals that had been described as ritual.

**Status: CONTESTED** (scholarly dispute; Isaac is the main named dissent from the ritual-treaty reading).

> **Why this matters for Juan's frame:** Isaac's argument comes from mainstream scholarship, and it **supports** the idea that elites manufactured religious justifications. It does *not* support a hidden trans-civilizational group. The elite in Isaac's account is the visible Tetzcocan and Mexica nobility, rationalizing in public.

### Claim 1.4: Child sacrifice was ordered by a secretive elite rather than by priests or the populace.

**Primary sources.** The same as above. The Nahuatl of FC Book 2 ch. 20 and the Atlcahualo chapter describe public calendrical rites at named mountains and at Pantitlan, a whirlpool in Lake Texcoco. Offering 48 lay inside the state's principal temple.

**Steelman.** Sacrifice in Tenochtitlan was controlled by the ruling class: the tlatoani, high priests, and the nobility. Children were purchased, which implies a procurement system run by people with wealth and authority. Isaac shows elites spinning religious justification. "Elite-directed" is fair.

**Counter-steelman.** "Elite-directed" is not "secretive." These were *public* state ceremonies, announced by the calendar, with crowds present (Sahagún's chapter describes processions). No source describes a hidden inner group behind the priesthood, and none links Mexica priests to any foreign organization.

**Status.**

- Elite-directed state religion: **DOCUMENTED**.
- Secretive group: **UNSUPPORTED**.
- Trans-civilizational group: **UNSUPPORTED**.

> **OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME:** "Death cults sacrificed children on behalf of a secretive elitist corrupt group spanning civilizations." The Mexica evidence supports "an elite-run state cult sacrificed children, publicly, for rain." Keep the frame beside that sentence, labeled. Do not merge them.

## 4. Section 2: Paris Catacombs

### Claim 2.1: The Catacombs are a municipal ossuary created for public health.

**Primary sources**

- **Official site**, Catacombes de Paris (Paris Musées), history page: https://www.catacombes.paris.fr/en/history. VERIFIED. It records:
  - first transfers of bones in 1785–1787 from the Saints-Innocents cemetery;
  - consecration as the "Paris Municipal Ossuary" on 7 April 1786;
  - opening to visitors by appointment from 1809;
  - a depth of about 20 m, 243 steps, and a 1.5 km circuit.
- **Louis-Étienne Héricart de Thury**, *Description des catacombes de Paris* (Paris: Bossange et Masson, 1815). Digital copy: e-rara https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-66665; also archive.org `descriptiondesc00thurgoog`. VERIFIED. The author was inspector-general of the quarries. The book documents:
  - 1780 complaints by residents of rue de la Lingerie about a mass grave opened at the end of 1779, holding more than 2,000 bodies, beside the Innocents;
  - the consecration "Le 7 avril suivant" by the abbés Motret, Mayet, and Asseline;
  - the inscription program, including "Arrête ! c'est ici l'empire de la mort !";
  - the creation of the quarry inspectorate;
  - a chapter on the Revolutionary period with crypts for victims of the riots of 28–29 Aug 1788 and of the September massacres. The printed French gives "2 et 3 septembre 1793", but the Latin gives MDCCXCII (1792). The massacres were in 1792, so the French line is a printing inconsistency.

**Steelman.** This is the documented origin: overflowing parish cemeteries, collapses and contamination, Enlightenment public-health policy, and the old limestone quarries reused to hold bones.

**Counter-steelman.** No serious historian disputes the origin. The only nuance is that the "6 million" figure often quoted for the remains is an estimate (not verified here; UNVERIFIED).

**Status: DOCUMENTED** (mainstream; no dissent found).

### Claim 2.2: Child sacrifice or elite ritual occurs or occurred in the Catacombs.

**Primary sources.** None found. Neither Héricart de Thury, nor the official site, nor any police or archival report I located documents ritual killing of children in the Catacombs or the wider quarry network.

**Steelman.** The network is huge (official accounts describe hundreds of kilometres of quarries; exact length UNVERIFIED here), mostly off-limits, and used by unofficial groups. Hidden spaces really have been found. In **August 2004** police found a fully equipped cinema and bar about 18–20 m under the Trocadéro / Palais de Chaillot, built by **La Mexicaine de Perforation** (part of the collective Les UX; spokesman Lazar Kunstmann). When police returned, the equipment was gone and a note read "Don't look for us." (*The Guardian*, 8 Sep 2004, https://www.theguardian.com/world/2004/sep/08/filmnews.france; UNVERIFIED journalism used to locate the event.) If an art collective could build a cinema unseen, the argument goes, the network can hide anything.

**Counter-steelman.**

- The 2004 case shows **secrecy without sacrifice**. The group was artistic. The police inquiry reportedly concerned electricity theft, not violence.
- The underground subculture of cataphiles is documented in journalism and ethnography. I found no allegation of child sacrifice that reached any court, police statement, or archive.
- The Lanning report is the relevant law-enforcement benchmark for organized ritual murder: no bodies, no trace evidence, no organized cult found (see §7).

**Status: UNSUPPORTED.**

### Claim 2.3: The Catacombs carry death symbolism and a subculture.

**Primary sources.** Héricart de Thury's inscriptions are memento mori ("Arrête ! c'est ici l'empire de la mort !"), and the official site describes bones arranged into decorative walls.

**Further claims:**

- Folklore of **Philibert Aspairt** (a Val-de-Grâce doorkeeper lost in 1793, found in 1804, buried in place): Wikipedia via search. UNVERIFIED.
- WWII use:
  - Colonel Rol-Tanguy's FFI command post under Place Denfert-Rochereau, 20–28 Aug 1944. Musée de la Libération page, https://www.museeliberation-leclerc-moulin.paris.fr/en/visit-museum/visite-du-pc-du-colonel-rol-tanguy, via search. UNVERIFIED as opened.
  - A German bunker under the Lycée Montaigne: Wikipedia via search. UNVERIFIED.

**Status:**

- Memento-mori design and the cataphile subculture: **DOCUMENTED**.
- Aspairt and WWII details: plausibly documented, but sources not opened here, so UNVERIFIED.
- The Catacombs as "the temple of an elite death cult": **UNSUPPORTED**.

> **OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME (if used):** "The Catacombs are a death-cult space for elites." Recommended handling: teach the Catacombs as a case study in how a public-health ossuary acquired an occult reputation. Real secrecy (the 2004 cinema) is not evidence of real sacrifice.

### 13.3 Steelman

Cultures can be modeled as replicating information packages (memes in Dawkins's 1976 sense; UNVERIFIED as opened) that spread through institutions as well as through births. Collins's points:

NCHS Data Brief 297 (Jan 2018; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-HE20_6200-PURL-gpo89906/pdf/GOVPUB-HE20_6200-PURL-gpo89906.pdf; VERIFIED): among US women aged 18–44 (2011–2015), "The average number of births among women aged 18–44 living in rural areas (1.56) was higher than the average for women in urban areas (1.28)," and 69.7% vs 58.4% had at least one birth.
   - *Material mechanisms explain much of the gap.* Lyman Stone, himself a pronatalist (IFS/Gallup, "More Crowding, Fewer Babies," June 2024; https://ifstudies.org/ifs-admin/resources/research/lymanstonefertilityhousingbrief-june.pdf; VERIFIED), finds: "When women live in smaller, more crowded houses, they have fewer children." He also finds that for 3+ bedroom units, "fertility is pretty much identical whether the unit is an apartment or a single-family home." That points to housing space, not ideology alone.

A. Wrigley 1967; UNVERIFIED as opened). That predates "wokeness" by centuries. "Parasitism" relabels urbanization plus migration.

Convergence is consistent with ordinary assimilation under shared material conditions. It doesn't require a predatory culture.
   - *Demographic transition theory* (Notestein and successors; UNVERIFIED as opened) links fertility decline to falling child mortality, women's education, urbanization, and contraception worldwide, including in societies with no "Hicksite" lineage (e.g., East Asia, which Stone discusses: Korea, Hong Kong).

4. **The Hicksite origin claim.**

5. **"Negative utilitarian" as a description of progressive ethics.**
