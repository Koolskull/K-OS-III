Prepared for KOOLSKULL (Juan). Internal research, not for publication. Compiled 2026-10-06.
Short names in brackets, like [NYT-obit], refer to entries in `SOURCES.md`.

**Status tags.** VERIFIED: two independent reliable sources, or a primary or official record. SUPPORTED: one reliable source. CLAIMED: an anecdote or retelling nobody has independently checked. UNVERIFIED: searched for, nothing found. CONTRADICTED: reliable sources say otherwise. NOT ADJUDICATED: the evidence conflicts and I am not ruling on it.
**Quote-medium tags.** (primary doc) = contemporaneous document or broadcast. (official) = estate, museum, Vincennes University (VU) or an awarding body. (press) = newspaper, encyclopedia or book relaying a quote. (fan) = fan or enthusiast site. (machine transcript) = my own speech-to-text.

## 2. Christian faith

**Denomination: NOT ADJUDICATED.**
- Protestant and Masonic:
  - [AmCath] (McClarey, 2009) and [CathOrg] both describe the family as Protestant.
  - Mason since Vincennes Lodge No. 1; 33rd degree in 1969; "58 year membership in the Masons and the Shriners" [RS-official].
- Catholic ties:
  - Parish records from the Old Cathedral, Vincennes, for his parents' 1906 marriage (museum timeline).
  - His children attended Catholic school.
  - Audience with Pius XII in 1957, and a visit to Lourdes.
  - He performed for "three Roman Catholic Popes" [VU].
- In 1957 he told TV Guide (press, via [TVAcad]): "I have been reading a lot of religious books… I think mine is to make people laugh."

**Sign-off: VERIFIED as a God-bless sign-off. The wording varies.**
- "Good night, and God bless." (press: [NYT-obit], [LAT-obit])
- "Good night and may God bless." ([Snopes], [RS-official])
- 1982 (press, via Wikipedia): "I don't use it as a gimmick. I mean it from the bottom of my heart."

**The Pledge of Allegiance monologue**
- **Air date: 14 Jan 1969, *The Red Skelton Hour*, CBS. VERIFIED** from three independent sources:
  - the official museum episode guide (episode 18.16);
  - a Scottish Rite Masonic Museum broadside dated "1969 January 14";
  - [Snopes].
- **Snopes rating: "True"** (David Mikkelson, 2002). VERIFIED.
- **Chart:** Columbia single on the Billboard Hot 100. Debuted at No. 96 on 3/15/1969 and **peaked at No. 44 on 4/12/1969** [Billboard]. VERIFIED.
- **Afterlife:**
  - 200,000 viewer requests to CBS, and a performance for Nixon (Hyatt via Wikipedia). SUPPORTED.
  - An FCC ruling that he had the "Right to Read His Pledge", answering a complaint by Anthony R. Martin-Trigona (museum timeline citing a 1969 newspaper headline). SUPPORTED, single source.
  - Christianity Today, July 1970 (primary doc): the "only significant reference to deity" at Honor America Day was Skelton's hope that "under God" would not get the Pledge "barred from the nation's schools."
- **Transcript:** the full Snopes text is in the Appendix.
  - It closes: "two words have been added to the Pledge of Allegiance: 'under God.' Wouldn't it be a pity if someone said that is a prayer, and that would be eliminated from schools too?"

**Is "Christian comedian" a fair label?**
- Fair as a description of the man: the sign-off, the "under God" remark, and the estate's "a man of deep faith" (official).
- Misleading as a genre: his act was secular clowning, not faith-themed comedy.
  - He mocked a televangelist (press, [NYT-obit]): "I bought Oral Roberts's Christmas record album but I couldn't play it. The hole healed up."
  - Writer Mark Evanier describes rehearsal "dirty hours" of "dirty joke after dirty joke" (fan/industry blog).
- The on-air clean rule is VERIFIED (press, [NYT-obit]): "I'd rather have people say, 'Boy, he's hokey, isn't he?' rather than, 'Who was the guy who told all those dirty jokes?'"
- More defensible label: "a clean, family comedian of public Christian and patriotic conviction."

## 3. Santa Clarita verification (a–e)

**Verdict: no evidence found** of a Skelton theater, room, piano or permanent display at any Santa Clarita college. An empty search is not proof of absence.

- **(a) College of the Canyons (COC) Performing Arts Center.**
  - COC is a public community college, not a Christian school.
  - The PAC is an "$18.3-million facility" that "opened in 2004", with 886 seats [COC-PAC]. A 2007 report gave 926 seats [DN-2007].
  - Skelton link: no evidence found.
- **(b) The Master's University**, a Christian school in Santa Clarita: no evidence found. The only "Skelton" hit is an unrelated name.
- **(c) CalArts, Newhall, Valencia:** no evidence found. His homes were in Bel Air, Palm Springs and Rancho Mirage, plus a ranch in the Anza Valley in Riverside County [LAT-2018].
- **(d) Vincennes University, Indiana: the theater with memorabilia.**
  - The Red Skelton Performing Arts Center was dedicated 24 Feb 2006. It has 850 seats, and its "grand foyer serves as a gallery for Red Skelton paintings, statues, and movie posters" [VU-PAC]. VERIFIED.
  - The museum opened 18 July 2013 [IIB].
  - In 2023 Lothian gave $4 million for a 7,200 sq ft art gallery [VU-2023].
- **(e) California Baptist University (CBU), Riverside: the best Christian-college lead.**
  - CBU is a Christian university about 80 miles from Santa Clarita.
  - A 2017 exhibit (May 30–Aug 31) showed 35 Skelton works donated by the Jim and Edna White family, including his "Mean Widdle Kid" trumpet and musical scores. Lothian attended [CBU-2017], [PE-2017].
  - In 2016 CBU launched a clean-comedy Comedic Arts degree developed with the Red Skelton Foundation and Lothian. Its courses include "Pantomime, Commedia & Physical Comedy" and "Comedy in Christian Ministries" [PE-2018].
  - Limits: the exhibit was temporary, it was not a theater, and no piano is mentioned.

**My hypothesis, not a finding:** the memory may blend VU's theater-with-memorabilia and CBU's Christian-college Skelton ties.

## 4. Underrated-legend steelman

- **Wetzsteon** (Village Voice 1977, via St. James/Maltin; press). Wording SUPPORTED; I did not check it against the Voice scan.
  > "a mime whose greatest success was on the radio. A folk humorist in the years when American entertainment was becoming urban… Red Skelton's career is a study in how to miss every trend that comes down the pike."
  - Read as a steelman: being out of step can mean undervalued, not weak.
- **Peer esteem** (press, VERIFIED as attributed):
  - Groucho Marx: "the most unacclaimed clown in show business" and "the logical successor to [Charlie] Chaplin".
  - Johnny Carson, his writer in 1953–54 [TVAcad]: "the last of the great physical performers."
  - David Rose [TVAcad]: "He is the greatest mime ever."
- **Dignity of characters:**
  - Leo Rosten, via Gehring (press): "a kindly respect for those who are not quite 'all there'… To imitate . . . [them] without malice or derision is quite a feat."
  - Clem came from affectionate imitations of Carl Hopper, a hearing-impaired boyhood friend Skelton protected [Gehring-IMH]. SUPPORTED (Hopper's daughter's account).
- **The two Freddie Christmas pieces:**
  - *Freddie and the Yuletide Doll*, 12/19/1961 (official guide): "A Christmas story in pantomime… No Monologue". VERIFIED.
  - "Cop and the Anthem", 12/20/1955: O. Henry, with dialogue. VERIFIED.
- **Craft:** he cut the biggest laughs to keep the show even (press, [TVAcad]): "Anything that got too big a laugh I took out". SUPPORTED, his own account.
- **Paintings:** more than 1,000 clown oils; canvases at $80,000 and up; about $2.5M a year from lithographs, by his own estimate [NYT-obit], [LAT-obit]. SUPPORTED.
- **Self-definition** (press, [NYT-obit]): "I just want to be known as a clown, because to me that's the height of my profession." VERIFIED.

## 5. Counter-steelman

- **Corny and dated.** VERIFIED that these critics said this:
  - NYT (press): "There were some who said he was too sentimental and not sophisticated enough."
  - MBC: "his passion for corny gags and slapstick".
  - St. James: his work "became more maudlin and he began losing his audience."
- **Formula.** SUPPORTED.
  - He recycled routines from "burlesque, vaudeville, radio, and motion pictures" [TVAcad] and deliberately flattened the laugh peaks.
  - Gehring: the Freddie restaurant routine is "borrowed directly from Chaplin's *Modern Times*."
- **Minstrelsy and blackface, stated plainly:**
  - In the medicine show "he advanced to doing a turn as a blackface singer" [LAT-obit]. SUPPORTED, single source.
  - Minstrel shows on his early résumé [NYT-obit].
  - A 1929 minstrel revue (museum timeline).
  - *Uncle Tom's Cabin* roles (Wikipedia).
  - I found no evidence of blackface on film or TV, but I did not watch the episodes. Common for the era, which explains but doesn't excuse it. The record is thin; don't inflate it.
- **B-movies.** SUPPORTED.
  - Comic relief in the Kildare films.
  - Post-war flops such as *The Show-Off* and *Merton of the Movies* [Indep], St. James.
- **The 1970 cancellation and the rural purge:**
  - VERIFIED: CBS dropped the show while it was still ranked seventh [MBC], [TVAcad]. Given reasons were costs, "upscale advertisers" [MBC] and not appealing to "younger viewers" [NYT-obit].
  - CLAIMED: Skelton blamed anti-war politics and the network's taste (Wikipedia).
  - SUPPORTED: the "rural purge" grouping is a mainstream retrospective frame.
- **Controversies.** All minor; no major scandal found.
  - 1947 NBC censorship (Wikipedia). SUPPORTED.
  - 1980 writers' lawsuit over his destroying the show tapes (Wikipedia; museum timeline citing AP). SUPPORTED.
  - The FCC Pledge complaint (§2).
  - Drinking in the early 1950s (Wikipedia). SUPPORTED.

## 6. Epistemic framing

- **Mainstream record** (Britannica, NYT, LA Times, TV Academy): a beloved, top-rated clown and pantomimist. Sentimental and old-fashioned. The cancellation was commercial.
- **The owner's dissenting synthesis (HIS inference):** Skelton is an underrated legend whose faith was integral and who belongs in K-OS's canon.
  - Partly supported by peer esteem (Groucho, Carson), cancellation despite ratings, and Wetzsteon's "missed every trend".
  - "Christian comedian" needs the qualification in §2.

## Appendix: Pledge of Allegiance, full text (Snopes transcription; matches my machine transcript of the 1969 clip)

Intro (Skelton quoting the teacher): "I've been listening to you boys and girls recite the Pledge of Allegiance all semester and it seems as though it's becoming monotonous to you. If I may, may I recite it and try to explain to you the meaning of each word?"

> I — Me, an individual, a committee of one.
> Pledge — Dedicate all of my worldly goods to give without self pity.
> Allegiance — My love and my devotion.
> To the flag — Our standard, Old Glory, a symbol of freedom. Wherever she waves, there's respect because your loyalty has given her a dignity that shouts freedom is everybody's job.
> United — That means that we have all come together.
> States — Individual communities that have united into 48 great states. Forty-eight individual communities with pride and dignity and purpose; all divided with imaginary boundaries yet united to a common purpose, and that's love for country.
> And to the republic — A state in which sovereign power is invested in representatives chosen by the people to govern. And government is the people, and it's from the people to the leaders, not from the leaders to the people.
> For which it stands, one nation — One nation, meaning "so blessed by God."
> Indivisible — Incapable of being divided.
> With liberty — Which is freedom, the right of power to live one's own life without threats, fear, or some sort of retaliation.
> And justice — The principle or quality of dealing fairly with others.
> For all — For all, which means, boys and girls, it's as much your country as it is mine.
> And now, boys and girls, let me hear you recite the Pledge of Allegiance: "I pledge allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America, and to the Republic for which it stands: one nation, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all."
> Since I was a small boy, two states have been added to our country, and two words have been added to the Pledge of Allegiance: "under God." Wouldn't it be a pity if someone said that is a prayer, and that would be eliminated from schools too?
