## What this lesson is for

Two tasks:

- Name the scriptural frame honestly: “foolishness” is the world’s verdict on the cross, not God’s.
- State the owner’s discernment point, labeled as his frame: sometimes appearing foolish is the cost of obedience; sometimes it is recklessness. The difference is discernment — and the alternative is creation.

Sources (VERIFIED unless marked otherwise):

- 1 Cor 1:18–31; 3:18; 4:8–13,10 (KJV/ESV/NIV via Bible Gateway) — the world calls the cross “foolishness”; “become a fool, that he may be wise”. [Bible Gateway]
- Acts 17:18 (“babbler”) and 2 Cor 11:16–12:10 (Paul’s “fool’s speech”). [Bible Gateway]

Positions in this lesson (each named and labeled):

## The world’s verdict, not God’s

- “For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness” (1 Cor 1:18 KJV). “The foolishness of God is wiser than men” (1:25). “God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise” (1:27 ESV). [Bible Gateway]
- “We are fools for Christ’s sake” (1 Cor 4:10 KJV). In context (4:8–13) Paul contrasts self‑satisfied Corinthians with apostles “as the offscouring of all things” (4:13). [Bible Gateway]
- “Let no one deceive himself. If anyone among you thinks that he is wise in this age, let him become a fool that he may become wise” (1 Cor 3:18 ESV). [Bible Gateway]

## A respectful contrast: Thích Quảng Đức (safety note included)

- Record (VERIFIED): Buddhist monk Thích Quảng Đức self‑immolated in Saigon on 11 June 1963 at Nguyễn Đình Chiểu × Cách Mạng Tháng Tám; Malcolm Browne’s photo won World Press Photo of the Year 1963; Browne and Halberstam won the 1964 Pulitzer for International Reporting; multiple further immolations followed that year. [World Press Photo archive; Pulitzer.org (1964 winners); research/fools-for-christ-research-report.md (Ngo 2023; timeline synthesis)]
- Religious reading (SUPPORTED/CONTESTED): Hoang Ngo (Kyoto Review of Southeast Asia, 2023) reads the act as a Lotus Sutra ch. 23 “Medicine King” offering — a Buddhist self‑offering more than a mere political tactic. Historians disagree about the extent of Ngô Đình Diệm’s anti‑Buddhist repression (e.g., Jacobs 2006 vs. Moyar 2006). [Kyoto Review 2023; survey per research report]
- Safety note: this curriculum never commends self‑harm or provoked death. If distress is acute, talk with someone you trust or a professional. Christian holy fools, in this track, are read as endurance, not self‑destruction.
