# What this track is for

Imagine you are standing in a dim reading room. The shelves are not labeled "truth" and "lies." They are labeled **sources**, **interpretations**, and **stories people tell themselves so they can sleep.** This track hands you a lamp—not a verdict.

K-OS already has an explicit rule for contested topics. Read [`docs/EPISTEMIC_STANCE.md`](../../../docs/EPISTEMIC_STANCE.md) if you have not. The short version: on topics where serious people disagree, the defaults present the mainstream account and the credible dissenting accounts, both sourced and attributed, and leave the conclusion to you. The project's closing line is older than any of us: "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good." (1 Thessalonians 5:21).

## What "deprogramming" means here

In popular culture the word is a war drum. Here it is a desk habit:

- Notice where a belief came from (media, school, family, algorithm, experience, institutional statement).
- Separate what can be checked against primary documents from interpretation and narrative.
- Keep the ability to hold competing accounts without collapsing into certainty or despair.

It is **not** a call to reject every institution, every program, or every conventional history. Real overreach has been documented. Ordinary error, bureaucratic incentive, and incomplete files also exist. Both can be true at once.

## The primary-source habit

Later lessons will send you to specific documents. The habit is always the same:

1. Read the primary text or declassified file when it is available.
2. Note what it actually says — and what it carefully does **not** say.
3. Only then open secondary interpretations: mainstream histories, dissenting books, contemporary commentary.
4. Keep a short note of where the accounts agree and where they diverge.

Useful permanent doors:

- [CIA Reading Room](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/)
- [National Security Archive](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/)
- Church Committee reports (1975–76) via National Archives and major libraries

{{illust:archive-scroll|Endless archive rows scrolling past — the feeling of FOIA, not the fantasy of one perfect folder.}}

You do not need to master these archives before starting. They are simply where later lessons send you when a claim needs an anchor.

## How the lessons are ordered

1. **Simple salvation teachings of Jesus** — a minimal, text-based spiritual baseline.
2. **Robert Monroe and out-of-body experience** — one documented line of consciousness research.
3. **MKUltra, Stargate, and declassified programs** — primary historical record of specific projects.
4. **Nikola Tesla and Walter Russell** — documented engineering and cosmology versus later claims.
5. **Jekyll Island, the Federal Reserve, and the American System tradition** — 1910 meeting, competing monetary stories, one living political-economy frame.

Each lesson starts plain, then expands. You can stop after the short version of any lesson.

## What this track is not

- Not a claim that every problem traces to one ethnicity, one religion, or one city.
- Not a claim that Israel, Muslim-majority states, or any single actor is the sole historical driver. Overlapping networks beat single-villain stories as a working model.
- Not a requirement that you accept the American System frame or any conspiracy literature. Those are interpretive traditions among others.
- Not medical, legal, or psychiatric advice. If you are dealing with distress, psychosis risk, or trauma, seek qualified help.

## How to use it

Read the short version first. If it is useful, open the primary sources. Compare them with whatever mainstream summary you already know. The point is the **habit of checking**, not a new orthodoxy.

> You are not here to join a side. You are here to keep a mind that can still read.
