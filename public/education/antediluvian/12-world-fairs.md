# World's Fairs as alleged capitals

World's Fairs and international expositions (London 1851, Chicago 1893, Paris 1889/1900, St. Louis 1904, and dozens more) produced some of the most photographed "impossible" architecture in modern history: white temporary cities, massive domes, lagoons, and sculptures that vanish within years. Mud-flood / Tartaria media often reads these fairs as **the last public face of a suppressed world civilization** — capitals revealed, then demolished. This lesson puts fair history and alternative readings on the same table.

## What mainstream fair history already says (and it is wild enough)

- Fairs were **state and corporate soft power**: empire, industry, arts, and "progress" on display.
- Many landmark buildings were **staff, plaster, and staff-and-wood**, built fast for a season.
- Demolition or fire often followed; a few structures were rebuilt permanent (e.g. fragments, museums).
- Photography and postcards made fairs feel eternal even when the plaster was already rotting.

Primary-adjacent doors:

- Official fair guides and plan books digitized by libraries
- Local historical society photo collections
- [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/) and national library expo holdings

Some fairs *did* host real technical marvels (electric lighting spectacles, new transit, materials science). Marvel is not automatically suppressed antediluvian physics.

## Method for fair claims

1. **Identify the fair** (year, city, official name).
2. **Find the building's materials** in contemporary engineering or guide literature.
3. **Check planned duration** — temporary by contract vs intended permanent.
4. **Separate** postcard colorization and modern AI upscales from period plates.
5. **Ask what happened to utilities** — generators, boilers, and wiring are usually documented even when romance is not.

## Keep / split / drop

| Claim | Keep? | Split? | Drop if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fairs built staggering temporary cities | Yes — documented | From "permanent Tartarian capitals" | Ignore staff/plaster literature |
| Elites used fairs for narrative control | Plausible soft-power reading | From free-energy demolition squads | No mechanism |
| Global style kinship | Yes — Beaux-Arts internationalism | From one buried empire | Style history unread |
| Fair photos show mud flood | Needs local stratigraphy | From postcard stains / flooding events | Every stain is global mud |

## Observe, don't convert

Standing in front of a Chicago 1893 photograph, it is rational to feel cognitive dissonance: how did they build that so fast? The productive answer path is **labor organization, materials science, and imperial budgets** — then, *if still unsatisfied*, extraordinary hypotheses. Skipping straight to Tartaria is the video-essay default, not a method requirement.

> Temporary does not mean trivial. Temporary also does not mean eternal capital buried by mud.
