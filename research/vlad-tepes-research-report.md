## 0. How to read this report

### 0.1 Labels

**Claim status** (shared convention with Death Cult Research):
- **DOCUMENTED**: supported by primary texts or contemporary documents, and accepted by mainstream specialists.
- **CONTESTED**: real evidence on more than one side, or named specialists disagree in print.
- **UNSUPPORTED**: no documentary evidence found.

**Source status:**
- **VERIFIED**: I opened the text itself, or a reliable record of it (a publisher or library catalogue page, or a scholarly edition or article quoting it), during this research.
- **UNVERIFIED**: known only from search snippets, secondary mention, or memory. Treat these as leads, not citations.

**Other markers:**
- **OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME** marks Juan's own framings. They sit *beside* the record and are never merged into it.
- **Quotes vs. paraphrase.** Text in quotation marks was actually seen at the stated URL or edition. Everything else is paraphrase, and I mark it as such where it might be mistaken for a quote.

### 0.2 How this fits `docs/EPISTEMIC_STANCE.md`

I read `docs/EPISTEMIC_STANCE.md` read-only, at repo commit `83fe41f1`. It does **not** define DOCUMENTED / CONTESTED / UNSUPPORTED labels. It does require the following, and this report follows all of them:
- attribute claims to named proponents;
- label mainstream and dissenting positions as such;
- point readers to primary sources;
- say plainly when a claim is fringe, and *still* present what its proponents argue;
- avoid conspiracy-as-identity framing in either direction;
- avoid false balance ("We do not present a debate as 50/50 when it isn't").

**Where the stance differs from the shared conventions, and how I handled it:**
1. **The stance leaves the final judgment to the reader.** The status labels are therefore offered as a *summary of the evidence*, not a verdict on anyone's faith or politics.
2. **The stance describes a "reader's role."** Each recommendation in §5 says what the reader can check for themselves.

I found no case where the two sets of rules actually conflict.

The stance's epigraph, 1 Thessalonians 5:21 ("Prove all things; hold fast that which is good."), is effectively the method of this report.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The core record of Vlad III is DOCUMENTED.**
   - Reigns: 1448, 1456–62 and 1476.
   - He was a hostage at the Ottoman court in the 1440s and spent roughly 1462–75 in Hungarian custody.
   - He died before 10 January 1477.
   - His father, Vlad II "Dracul", was admitted to the Order of the Dragon at Nuremberg c. 8 Feb 1431 (Rezachevici 1999).
2. **The anti-Ottoman record is real and strong.**
   - In 1461–62 he refused tribute, killed or captured the Ottoman envoys Hamza Bey and Thomas Katabolenos, and raided the Danube line. His own letter of 11 Feb 1462 claims about 23,884 dead.
   - He fought Mehmed II's 1462 invasion, including a night attack on the sultan's camp in June 1462.
   - Contemporaries across Latin Christendom treated him as a front-line anti-Ottoman prince (Pop 2012; Simon 2020).
3. **The "Christian hero" label is CONTESTED, and should be presented as OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME.** It rests on several things:
   - a real Orthodox ruler, founder of the Comana monastery (27 Sept 1461) and donor to Mount Athos (Rezachevici 2002);
   - a son of a crusading order's knight;
   - a man who fought the sultan.
   
   Against it:
   - Most of his documented victims were **Christians**: Wallachian boyars, Transylvanian Saxon merchants and villagers, and Hungarian subjects. Harai (2025) says this explicitly.
   - He came to power in 1448 and 1456 with Ottoman backing, and paid tribute in 1456–58.
   - The pope who knew him best, Pius II, headed his chapter on Vlad with the words "immanis atque nefanda crudelitas" (monstrous and abominable cruelty).
   - Even the Russian *Skazanie*, which calls him "a Christian voivode of the Greek faith", still makes the name "Dracula" mean "the Devil".
4. **The numbers are the weakest part of the popular story.**
   - "Forest of the impaled": 20,000 per Chalkokondyles; 40,000 per Nicholas of Modruš; 100,000 per Gabriele Rangoni.
   - The most recent quantitative study (Harai, *TRHS* 2025) estimates **1,600–1,700** in that forest and **about 2,000 impaled in total**.
   - Harai's own words: "These figures certainly do not downplay the brutality of Vlad's rule".
   - Status: impalement as state terror is **DOCUMENTED**; the large figures are **CONTESTED**.
5. **"Defended against Muslim oppression tyrannically run for hundreds of years" is half right.**
   - Ottoman pressure was real: raids from the 1390s, akıncı warfare, the child-levy (devshirme) in the Balkans, and an Ottoman legal view of Wallachia as *dar al-harb* until conquest (Panaite).
   - But in Vlad's day Wallachia was a **tributary Christian principality with its own princes and church**. Tributary status was formalized only in 1417. "Hundreds of years" fits the later period (16th–19th centuries, including Phanariot rule 1714–1821) better than Vlad's own lifetime.
   - Status: **CONTESTED as a description of 1456–62.**
6. **"Dismissing Vlad = dismissing Christian genocide" is not a historical finding.** It is a moral and theological argument.
   - "Genocide" was coined in 1944 (Lemkin) and defined in law in 1948.
   - I found no named historian applying it to Vlad's Wallachia.
   - Recommendation: **OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME**, presented next to the documented record of Ottoman violence and of Vlad's violence against Christians. Never conflate it with the 1915–23 genocides.
7. **"Christians destroy the enemy when the Lord tells them to" is theology, with a real pedigree on both sides.**
   - For: the *herem* texts (Deut 7 and 20; Josh 6; 1 Sam 15), Augustine (*Contra Faustum* XXII.74–75), Aquinas (II-II q.40), and Urban II as reported by Fulcher.
   - Against: Matt 5:38–48; Rom 12:17–21; Matt 26:52; Tertullian; Origen.
   - Even the just-war writers do not license cruelty. Augustine names "revengeful cruelty" as one of "the real evils in war".
   - Status: **OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME**, taught as a live intra-Christian debate.
8. **The death-cult and secret-elite angles are UNSUPPORTED for Vlad.**
   - Harai shows that impalement was a borrowed judicial and terror punishment, used by the Hungarian crown (*jus gladii*), the Ottomans, Skanderbeg, the Knights of Rhodes and a Venetian admiral. It was not a ritual.
   - The only real "order" in Vlad's story is the **Order of the Dragon (1408)**. It was public, royal and Christian, under St George, and aimed against the Ottomans and Hussites. Vlad III himself never used its emblem (Rezachevici 1999).
9. **Addenda.** No serious historian links Vlad to Freemasonry, the Illuminati, Venice-as-conspiracy, the Rothschilds, Nephilim or archons.
   - Chronology alone rules out the first, second and fourth: Grand Lodge 1717; Illuminati 1776–85; the Rothschild network in the 19th century.
   - Venice *was* a real, documented anti-Ottoman correspondent in 1462.
   - The Rothschild trope has a documented antisemitic lineage (Toussenel 1845; the "Satan" pamphlet of 1846).
   - Icke (1999) mentions Vlad only by way of Laurence Gardner and the "Draco" wordplay. In the online text of Springmeier's *Bloodlines* I found no mention of Vlad.
   - Charles III's 2011 remark is documented, but the family tree behind it has an unsourced link and the expected DNA share is about 0.0015% (§7b).
   - Juan's own dictated words in `docs/CREATIVITY_AS_THE_WAY_OUT.md` already say finance is "not exclusively the work of machinations of Rothschild or Illuminati bloodlines or Nephilim worshippers".

## 3. Claim-by-claim

### C1. Basic biography: DOCUMENTED (with uncertain edges)

**Record**
- Harai (2025) gives Vlad's dates as "c. 1431–76" and places him at the sultan's court "(1442–7)", visiting "to pay annual tribute (1456–8)". He also places him in Moldavia (1449–52) and Transylvania (1452–6).
- Rezachevici (1999) gives the reigns as "1448, 1456-1462, 1476".
- Death: Rezachevici (2002) writes that he was killed "before January 10, 1477, as a result of a plot of the boyars loyal to Basarab Laiota, helped by the Turks", and that his head was sent to Constantinople.
- Burial:
  - Rezachevici shows that the Snagov burial "tradition" dates from the 19th century. The 1933 excavation found no tomb under the slab attributed to Vlad.
  - He argues that **Comana**, which Vlad founded (land grant of 27 Sept 1461), is "the most plausible" burial site, while calling it "of course a hypothesis".

**Accounts of the death differ**
- The *Skazanie* (Stanford translation) says he was killed by his own men, who mistook him for a Turk ("Five more lances pierced him").
- Rezachevici reads it as a boyar plot.
- *Paraphrase:* Stephen of Moldavia's letter of January 1477 is commonly cited as reporting the death and the massacre of Vlad's Moldavian escort. I saw this only in a search summary: UNVERIFIED.

**Birthplace**
- Sighișoara is the traditional birthplace. Rezachevici documents Vlad II striking coins at Sighișoara. I found no primary document of Vlad III's birth there, so the birthplace is UNVERIFIED.

**Steelman (a full life story):** the dates and offices are secure from charters, letters and foreign chronicles.

**Counter-steelman:** much of the "life" (the boyar purge, the anecdotes) comes from hostile narrative sources. Cazacu (Brill 2017; table of contents VERIFIED) and Harai both separate charters from stories.

### C2. The name: DOCUMENTED

Rezachevici (1999, JDS 1; full text read) says the following:
- "Draculea (Dracula) was a popular nickname for Vlad Dracul, meaning a person belonging to the Order of the Dragon."
- Vlad II received the Order "at Nürnberg around February 8, 1431".
- Originally, "in his father's days, 'Dracul' did not have a malevolent meaning".
- The diabolical reading spread through Vlad III's enemies:
  - Dan III, 1459–60, called him "the devil that is Vlad Voievod";
  - Nicholas of Modruš wrote "their tyrant Dracul, a name which they [Romanians] use for the Devil";
  - the 1486 Russian tale says "Dracula in Romanian, and in our language - devil".
  
  These are Rezachevici's renderings, through Romanian editions.
- Rezachevici also notes that "out of all the Romance languages, the Romanian language was the only one in which 'draco' has the meaning of an evil spirit, demon or devil" (citing Vasile Pârvan).

**Teaching point:** the name moved from a knightly St-George badge to a devil-pun, and the move was made by Vlad's enemies. That is a clean, documented case study in reputation-making.

### C3. Brașov, 1459–60: DOCUMENTED event, CONTESTED scale

- **Record.** Harai cites Dan III's letter of 2 Apr 1459 giving **41** impaled Brașov merchants. The German pamphlet says **600**. The Melk chronicle reports 200 impaled near St James's chapel, Brașov. *(Paraphrase of Harai.)*
- **Steelman (Vlad's side).** The Brașov and Burzenland Saxons were sheltering rival claimants: Dan III wrote from there. Their trade privileges were also a lever of power. Romanian historians such as Andreescu and Stoicescu (as summarized by Light 2007) argued that Saxon and Hungarian enemies exaggerated the cruelty.
- **Counter-steelman.** The victims were Christian merchants and villagers, not Ottomans. Harai's census evidence for Amlaș, and a Corvinus deed of 3 Dec 1462 recording depopulated Făgăraș villages, show real killing of Christian subjects.

### C4. The Easter boyar purge: outline DOCUMENTED, details legendary or CONTESTED

- **Record.** Chalkokondyles (Kaldellis tr., 9.104 area, as reproduced by medievalists.net) says Vlad summoned "each of the distinguished men of the realm who, it was believed, had committed treason… He killed them all by impalement, them and their sons, wives, and servants". It adds: "they say that in a short time he killed twenty thousand men, women and children."
- The **Easter setting** and the 500 boyars come from the German pamphlet tradition. The detail of forced labour at Poenari is Wallachian chronicle and folklore: Light (2007) notes the chronicles have him build Poenari "using forced laborers from Târgoviște".
- **Harai** reports that Trow and Cazacu estimate **40–50** victims: the Târgoviște hall measured about 12×7 m, and 11 of 23 councillors vanish from the charters.
- **Steelman.** Boyar factions had killed Vlad's father and brother in 1447. Chalkokondyles frames the purge as revenge on traitors and as state-building ("utterly revolutionized the affairs of Wallachia").
- **Counter-steelman.** The killing of wives, children and servants, which Chalkokondyles reports, is collective punishment of Christians. The rule of thumb for teaching: names and charters show a purge happened; the Easter date and the 500 are literature.

### C5. The Danube raid, winter 1461–62: DOCUMENTED, figure CONTESTED

- **Record.** Vlad's letter to Matthias of 11 Feb 1462 survives in Munich and Wolfenbüttel copies (Harai; edited by Bogdan 1896; Cazacu pp. 140–142):
  - Munich gives a total of 23,884, but its local tallies sum to 20,099.
  - Wolfenbüttel gives 23,889, against tallies of 22,879.
  - Harai quotes the letter's method: "And the figures given above represent the number of dead whose heads and 'signs' were brought to our officers who were placed everywhere."
  - Mihailović (per Harai) says the "signs" were noses.
  - A Venetian report of March 1462 gives 21,660.
  - **None of these sources mention impalement.**
- **Steelman.** This is the strongest "anti-Ottoman warrior" evidence: a tributary prince openly attacking the sultan's Danube line in the year after the Mantua crusade appeal.
- **Counter-steelman.**
  - Chalkokondyles (as reproduced) says he killed "everyone, women and children included" and burned houses.
  - The Danubian population included Bulgarian Christians. *Paraphrase:* the famous "Turks and Bulgarians" wording of the letter appears in popular sources, but I did not see it in a verified text: UNVERIFIED.
  - The *Skazanie* has Vlad sparing and resettling "those who were Christians".

### C6. The Night Attack, June 1462: DOCUMENTED, date CONTESTED

- **Record.** Chalkokondyles (via medievalists.net) describes the surprise attack on the camp. It "failed to sow panic" and the Wallachians withdrew before dawn; that phrase is medievalists.net's paraphrase of the source.
- **Date.** Pop (2012, Romanian version read) dates it to the night of 17–18 June. A search summary of Gheorghe (2017) gives 16/17 June from William Wey's account and calls that source second- or third-hand (UNVERIFIED).
- **Teaching recommendation:** "mid-June 1462 (16/17 or 17/18 June depending on the source)."
- **Steelman.** It was a bold attempt on the sultan's life and camp by a much smaller force. Italian dispatches reported Ottoman setbacks (Pop: Milanese envoy Antonio Guidobono, citing the Venetian secretary in Buda, "Pietro de Thomasi").
- **Counter-steelman.** Militarily it failed. Mehmed advanced, Radu took the throne in August 1462 with boyar support (Simon), and Vlad fled to Hungary.

### C7. The forest of the impaled: field DOCUMENTED, 20,000 CONTESTED

- **Primary text (Kaldellis tr., DOML 2014, 9.104; quoted as reproduced by medievalists.net):** "The sultan's army entered into the area of the impalements, which was seventeen stades long and seven stades wide. There were large stakes there on which, as it was said, about twenty thousand men, women, and children had been split… There were infants too affixed to their mothers on the stakes, and birds had made their nests in their entrails."
  - Note Chalkokondyles' hedge: "as it was said".
  - Mehmed's reported reaction: "a man who had done such things was worth much".
- **Eyewitness.** Per Harai, citing Gheorghe 2017 and Andreescu, Enveri was on the 1462 campaign and saw a field of impaled Turks, but gives no number.
- **Other figures in the sources:**
  - 40,000: Nicholas of Modruš, papal legate, 1463–4
  - 100,000: Gabriele Rangoni, 1476
  - 4,000: rumour in Balbi's letter, 28 July 1462
- **Modern doubts.**
  - Kreuter (2018) calls the forest "an exaggerated literary episode".
  - Harai (2025) estimates "1,600–1,700 executed people made up the 'forest of the impaled' at Târgoviște rather than the 20,000", and concludes: "These figures certainly do not downplay the brutality of Vlad's rule, but, contrary to the popular legend and the sensationalist claims of some contemporary chroniclers, impalement was not a principal tool for mass killing."
- **Steelman (hero case).** Even at about 1,700, the field shows Vlad deliberately using terror against an invading army and its captives, and that it worked psychologically. Mehmed's admiring remark is the hero case's favourite line.
- **Counter-steelman.** The number is inflated by roughly 12× in the most quoted source. The "infants on mothers" detail is Chalkokondyles' hearsay. Teaching the 20,000 as fact goes against the stance's rule to "point readers to primary sources", since the primary source itself hedges.

### C8. The pamphlet debate: DOCUMENTED propaganda, scale debated

**The German text**
- Repertorium Fontium / Geschichtsquellen (VERIFIED) describes *Geschichte Dracole Waide* as 35 short episodes "um Vlad zu diffamieren" (written to defame Vlad). It was probably composed c.1462 at Matthias Corvinus's court, likely from a Latin original that also underlies Pius II's *Commentarii* and Ebendorfer's *Cronica regum Romanorum*.
- Michel Beheim's poem versifies it (Beheim's text is an appendix in Cazacu 2017, pp. 317–346).
- Prints:
  - Nuremberg 1488 (Ayrer)
  - Nuremberg c.1489 (Wagner)
  - Bamberg 1491
  - Leipzig 1493
  - Augsburg 1494
  - Strasbourg 1500
  - Lübeck c.1490 (Low German)
- Cazacu (1981) argued for a lost Vienna incunable of 1463. That is CONTESTED; no Vienna press is attested in 1463 (search summary; Persée record seen).

**Scholars' positions**
- *Florescu & McNally* (1972, 1989) used the pamphlets heavily and portrayed Vlad as a tyrant. Light (2007) calls *In Search of Dracula* "relentless in its portrayal of Vlad as an absolute tyrant".
- *Treptow* (2000) is a more sympathetic synthesis from the Center for Romanian Studies (catalogue VERIFIED; content not read).
- *Andreescu* (1976) and *Stoicescu* (1976): per Light, they argued that "many of the reports of the Impaler's cruelty were exaggerations by his Saxon and Hungarian enemies", while not denying he was "an exceptionally harsh leader".
- *Cazacu* and *Kreuter* identify the exaggeration (per Harai).
- *Harai* (2025) quantifies it: a plausibility rate of 7–10%, or 8–8.5%.
- *Simon* (2020) places the German texts in anti-Hunyadi and imperial politics (Frederick III and Albert VI).

**Steelman (exaggeration).** These were hostile sources with motives: to justify Vlad's arrest and Matthias's failure to crusade with the papal subsidies.

**Counter-steelman (they were not invented).** Independent Ottoman, Greek, Russian and Italian sources also report impalements. Harai's point is that "the stakes were real and the number of victims does matter".

### C9. The forged-letters controversy: CONTESTED

- **Record.** Vlad's letter to Mehmed "written at Rhotel" (7 Nov 1462), offering submission and help against Hungary, survives only in Pius II's *Commentarii* XI.12. Simon (2020) calls it "the only extant proof of his treason", "left unrecorded by all other parties".
- Pius's chapter title: "Iohannis Dragule immanis atque nefanda crudelitas, eiusque in regem Hungarie deprehensa perfidia, et tandem captivitas". Roughly: John Dragula's monstrous and abominable cruelty, his treachery against the king of Hungary detected, and at last his capture.
- **The debate.** Simon says the historiographic debate since the 1890s has run between "Matthias' manifest forgery or Vlad's undeniable treason". Simon also notes that the stated official reason for the arrest was cruelty.
- **Hero steelman.** A single-source letter that conveniently excused Matthias pocketing crusade money looks forged. This is the standard Romanian position.
- **Counter-steelman.** Princes caught between empires routinely hedged. Vlad himself had come to power with Ottoman backing in 1448 and 1456.
- **Teaching recommendation:** present it as an open question.

### C10. "Vlad was a Christian hero": OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME

**Evidence for**
- He was an Orthodox prince. The *Skazanie* opens with "a Christian voivode of the Greek faith". He founded Comana (1461) and a church at Târgșor (1461), and made "substantial donations to Mount Athos" (Rezachevici 2002).
- Snagov is **not** his foundation. Rezachevici: "there are no documents whatsoever regarding the monastery of Snagov from Vlad Tepes himself".
- He was the son of a Dragon knight. The Order's statutes aimed "at defending the cross and at the destruction of its enemies … with the help of St. George" (Rezachevici's paraphrase of the 1408 statute).
- He fought the sultan in 1461–62 when other Christian powers hesitated. Venice, the papacy and Milan followed his war closely (Pop; Simon).
- Later Romanian nationalism (19th century onward) made him a just, anti-Ottoman prince. Eminescu's line "[W]hy do you not come Lord Țepeș?" is quoted in Light 2007.

**Evidence against**
- *Victims.* Harai: "Vlad used collective impalements against Wallachians (both in Wallachia and in the duchies of Amlaș and Făgăraș), his former Saxon allies (especially in Burzenland), and even his Ottoman suzerains".
- *Ottoman client.* He took power in 1448 and 1456 with Ottoman backing, was a hostage at the Ottoman court, and paid annual tribute in 1456–58 (Harai).
- *How contemporary Catholics saw him.*
  - Pius II: "immanis atque nefanda crudelitas".
  - Matthias arrested him and cited cruelty.
  - Nicholas of Modruš used the devil pun.
- *His brother.* Radu, his brother and Mehmed's candidate, won the boyars in August 1462. Chalkokondyles has Radu tell them that Vlad was "a most unholy man who has brought such harm upon Wallachia" (quoted as reproduced by medievalists.net).
- *Denomination.* He is said to have converted to Catholicism as the price of release. The *Skazanie* says he accepted "the Latin faith" after 12 years at Visegrád. That is a single, hostile, Orthodox source: CONTESTED. Cazacu (2017) treats the exile (pp. 164–198, TOC), but I did not read that chapter.
- *Nationalist construction.* Boia (2001) and Light (2007) show how the hero image was built. Light argues the Ceaușescu-era cult is **overstated**: "while Vlad was clearly held in high esteem in Communist Romania, he was very much a secondary hero". He also found no Ceaușescu speech in 1976 mentioning Vlad.

**Verdict:** He was a Christian prince and an anti-Ottoman fighter, both DOCUMENTED. "Christian hero" as a moral category is OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME, and needs the victim record placed next to it.

### C11. "Defended against Muslim oppression tyrannically run for hundreds of years": partly DOCUMENTED, partly CONTESTED

**Record (Panaite, Princeton *Mapping Eastern Europe*; VERIFIED)**
- First Ottoman raids came in the 1390s; Bayezid I campaigned in 1395.

**"16 times removed" vs "granduncle": a contradiction to teach.**
- *Daily Mail* (Hardman): "Charles is Vlad the Impaler's great-grandson 16 times removed."
- Ancient Origins (Richard Clements, 12 June 2024; opened): "Vlad the Impaler is a 16-times great-granduncle to King Charles III… This means that Vlad is not a direct ancestor but rather a distant uncle many generations removed."
- A **granduncle is collateral**: the sibling of a direct ancestor. If Vlad is a granduncle, Charles descends from one of Vlad's siblings, not from Vlad.
- "Great-grandson … removed" implies *direct* descent. ("Removed" is properly a term for cousins.) Charles's own words and Snopes both claim *direct* descent.

### 7b.3 Claim: Charles carries a meaningful share of Vlad's blood or DNA (the owner's brief: "under 0.1%")

**Status:**
- "Under 0.1%" is technically true but overstates the expectation by about 65×.
- A meaningful genetic share is **UNSUPPORTED**.
- The most likely amount of Vlad's autosomal DNA in Charles is **zero**.

**Working:**
1. **Generations.** Count down the Telex chain (§7b.2) and then the documented upper part: Vlad (gen 0) → Mihnea (1) → [intermediate] (2) → Petru (3) → Maria (4) → Zsuzsanna (5) → Gábor (6) → Caspar (7) → Ágnes K. (8) → Gregor (9) → Ágnes I. (10) → Claudine (11) → Francis (12) → Mary (13) → George VI (14) → Elizabeth II (15) → Charles (16).
   - That makes Vlad Charles's ancestor at generation **16**, a "14× great-grandfather".
   - Some versions add a generation (17).
   - The *Daily Mail*'s "16 times removed" and Ancient Origins' "16-times great-granduncle" use the number loosely.
2. **Ancestral slots.** A person has 2 parents, 4 grandparents, and 2^n ancestral slots at generation n.
   - 2^16 = **65,536**
   - 2^17 = 131,072
3. **Expected autosomal share from one slot.** The expected share halves each generation, so it is 1/2^n.
   - 1/65,536 ≈ 0.0000153 ≈ **0.0015%**
   - 1/131,072 ≈ 0.00076%
   - Against the brief: 0.1% ÷ 0.0015% ≈ **65**. The expected share is about 65 times smaller than "under 0.1%" at 16 generations, and about 130 times smaller at 17.
4. **Pedigree collapse.** Noble and royal families intermarried, so one person can fill several slots, and 65,536 slots hold far fewer distinct people.
   - If Vlad filled two slots (Snopes's "two separate ways"), the expected share doubles to about 0.003%, still about 30× below 0.1%.
   - Collapse raises the expectation only in proportion to the number of genuine paths, and none of the claimed paths is proven.
5. **The probability of inheriting nothing.** DNA passes down in chunks, not as a smooth fraction.
   - Source: geneticist Graham Coop, "How many genetic ancestors do I have?", gcbias.org, 11 Nov 2013 (opened). He gives the probability of inheriting zero autosomal material from a particular ancestor *k* generations back as approximately **exp(−(22+33(k−1))/2^(k−1))**.
   - His rationale: "you have 22 autosomes, with roughly 33 recombination events per generation".
   - He notes that by about 9 generations it is already "quite likely that a specific ancestor contributed zero of your autosomal material".
   - *k* = 16: (22 + 33×15)/2^15 = 517/32,768 ≈ 0.0158. P(zero) ≈ e^−0.0158 ≈ **98.4%**.
   - *k* = 17: (22 + 33×16)/2^16 = 550/65,536 ≈ 0.0084. P(zero) ≈ **99.2%**.
   - The arithmetic is mine, applying Coop's formula.
   - Even if the paper genealogy were correct, each path would give only about a 1–2% chance that *any* of Vlad's autosomal DNA reached Charles.
   - The route also does not run along Charles's direct male line (Y chromosome) or his direct female line (mtDNA). For example, it passes from Claudine to her son Francis and back to a daughter, Mary.

**Steelman.** Genealogical descent is a fact about lineage and records, not about DNA. Royal identity has always been reckoned on paper.

**Counter-steelman.** Exactly so. That makes it purely a claim about *paperwork*, and the paperwork has a missing link (§7b.2). Talk of "Vlad's blood" adds nothing beyond that.

### 8.3 Theological tension

- **Vlad's tradition.** Vlad was an Orthodox prince (*Skazanie*: "of the Greek faith").
- **Gen 6:1–4** (KJV): "the sons of God saw the daughters of men… There were giants in the earth in those days… mighty men which were of old, men of renown."
- **1 Enoch 6** (R. H. Charles tr., sacred-texts) develops this: the angels "saw and lusted after them… they were in all two hundred; who descended … on the summit of Mount Hermon". 1 Enoch is canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, but not in the Romanian or Greek Orthodox canon (a standard fact, UNVERIFIED in this session).
- **Gnostic archons.** The *Hypostasis of the Archons* (Layton tr., Gnostic Society Library) makes the creator a blind chief ruler: "It is I who am God; there is none apart from me… 'You are mistaken, Samael' – which is, 'god of the blind'." Its rulers try to defile Eve and Norea. Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* I.30, New Advent) reports the parallel Ophite system ("Ialdabaoth… exclaimed, I am father, and God") **as heresy**. Irenaeus is a Father honoured by the Orthodox Church.
- **The tension.** An archon or Nephilim overlay on Vlad would make him either:
  - a bearer of a demonic bloodline, which contradicts his documented Orthodox self-presentation and church-founding; or
  - a hero against archons, which borrows a theology that his own church condemned as heresy, in which the God of Genesis is a blind demiurge.

  Either way, the overlay conflicts with the Christian-hero frame it is meant to support.

**Lesson recommendation:** **OWNER'S INTERPRETIVE FRAME**, UNSUPPORTED as history about Vlad. If used, make it a *method* lesson: "How a knightly badge became 'Draco reptilians': tracing a claim from Rezachevici's statute to Icke's book."
