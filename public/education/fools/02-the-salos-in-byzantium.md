## What this lesson is for

The Byzantine roots. What the texts say, who wrote them, when, and where hagiography and history part company.

Named positions:

- P1 Orthodox hagiographic: the holy fool feigns madness as hidden asceticism and charity.
- P3 Academic-historical: Lives are literary constructions; performance read by an audience (Ivanov).
- P4 Psychological/psychiatric: some cases may reflect illness later sanctified; retrospective diagnosis is contested.

Status tags appear with claims (VERIFIED / SUPPORTED / HAGIOGRAPHY / CONTESTED / NOT ADJUDICATED).

---

## Symeon of Emesa (DOCUMENTED text; CONTESTED history)

- Text and author: Life by Leontius, bishop of **Neapolis (Cyprus)**, 7th c. [Krueger 1996 — University of California Press; VERIFIED bibliographic details]
- Content (HAGIOGRAPHY): dragging a dead dog; pelting women with nuts in church; walking naked; using the women’s bath; converting heretics, Jews, prostitutes, actors; “mocking the world.” [Krueger trans.]
- Literary construction: Krueger shows Leontius models episodes on **Diogenes the Cynic**. The Life is a crafted late‑antique city story as much as a dossier. [Krueger — SUPPORTED]

---

## Andrew of Constantinople (CONTESTED)

- Text: Life attributed to “Nikephoros,” priest of Hagia Sophia; edited by **Lennart Rydén** (Studia Byzantina Upsaliensia 4, 1995). Composition: mid‑to‑late **10th century**; setting is fictional; Andrew’s historicity is doubtful. [Rydén — VERIFIED bibliographic; historicity CONTESTED]
- Tradition: vision of the Theotokos’s protecting veil (Blachernae) as origin of the feast of Protection (*Pokrov*) (HAGIOGRAPHY).
- Popular survey: JSTOR Daily (Amelia Soth, 2024) describes Andrew “pretending to be drunk,” sleeping with stray dogs, drinking from puddles (PRESS — SUPPORTED as summary).

---

## Desert roots and Church suspicion

- Palladius, Lausiac History 34: a nun feigns madness and does the dirtiest work; when honored, she flees (DOCUMENTED primary).
- Sayings of the Desert Fathers (Antony 25): “A time is coming when men will go mad, and when they see someone who is not mad, they will attack him…” (DOCUMENTED primary).
- Council in Trullo (692), canon 60: those who pretend demonic possession incur penance (DOCUMENTED; shows institutional suspicion of performance without sanctity).
