# When the sky fell — Younger Dryas

Around **12,900–11,700 years ago**, the Northern Hemisphere returned to near-glacial cold for roughly a millennium. That interval is the **Younger Dryas**. Mainstream paleoclimate treats it as a real climatic event. Alternative deep-history media often treats it as the moment a high culture died — a sky-fall, a flood pulse, or both.

This lesson maps both frames. You decide how much weight each carries.

{{illust:sky-rupture|Comet grit and a broken horizon — catastrophe as field, not cartoon.}}

## Short version (mainstream)

The Younger Dryas is documented in ice cores, pollen records, and paleolimnology. A leading mechanism hypothesis has long been **freshwater disruption of Atlantic overturning circulation** after ice-sheet meltwater floods (the "shutdown" storyline, still debated in detail). Whatever the trigger, the cooling is not a YouTube invention: it is a named stratigraphic and climatic interval with a large literature.

Key doors:

- [NOAA — Younger Dryas overview](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-11/2%20The%20Younger%20Dryas%20-Final-OCT%202021.pdf) (or current NCEI paleoclimate pages)
- Ice-core literature via [NGRIP / Greenland ice core projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Greenland_Ice_Core_Project) as a starting index

## The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis (YDIH)

A dissenting research cluster argues that **cosmic airbursts or impacts** around the YD onset triggered wildfires, dust loading, megafaunal stress, and abrupt cooling. Evidence offered includes:

- nanodiamonds, magnetic spherules, high-temperature melt glass (claimed at some sites)
- a proposed "black mat" organic-rich layer at terminal Pleistocene sites
- platinum and related geochemical anomalies in some records

Proponents publish in peer-reviewed venues; critics publish rebuttals in the same ecosystems. This is **not** "science vs YouTube" only — it is an **active intramural fight**.

## How alternative media uses it

Channels in the antediluvian orbit often treat YDIH as:

1. a **dating anchor** for a lost advanced culture (~13 ka),
2. a **mechanism** for global flood myths,
3. a bridge to **Göbekli Tepe**, coastal inundation, and (more loosely) anomalous maps that supposedly record ice-free polar coasts.

Watch for **clock merging**: Plato's "9,000 years before Solon," biblical flood calendars, and 12.8 ka proxies are not the same clock unless *you* do the conversion work and show it.

Creators such as Robert Sepehr and adjacent Atlantis-oriented programs often narrate YD-scale catastrophe as civilizational death. Treat that as **interpretation layered on proxies**, not as the ice core itself speaking. Occult and Theosophical lineages had catastrophe chronologies long before YDIH papers existed; modern impact literature sometimes gets retrofitted into those older mythic schedules.

## Critical counterpoints (read them)

Critics of YDIH argue:

- markers can form by terrestrial wildfire and other non-impact processes,
- site selection and replication problems,
- chronological scatter,
- megafaunal extinction as multi-causal (humans + climate).

A healthy read: hold **both** the pro-impact papers and the skeptical reviews open in two tabs. Note what would change your mind.

## Observe, don't convert

| Claim type | Example | Status habit |
|---|---|---|
| Event exists | YD cooling | Strongly evidenced as climate interval |
| Mechanism A | AMOC disruption | Mainstream family of models |
| Mechanism B | Cosmic impact/airburst | Contested research program |
| Culture story | Advanced world dies at 12.8 ka | Speculative narrative on top of either mechanism |

> The ice remembers cold. Interpreters remember stories. Keep the difference labeled.
