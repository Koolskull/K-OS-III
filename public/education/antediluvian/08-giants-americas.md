# Giants of the Americas and the old world

Giant humans sit at the intersection of **Enochic Nephilim**, **Indigenous and settler folklore**, **19th-century newspaper hoaxes**, and modern video essays that claim museums "disappeared the bones." Mr Mythos–style and broader alternative channels often treat giants as the **body** of the Watcher story — the physical residue of a pre-flood order. This lesson separates **text**, **folklore**, **documented archaeology**, and **viral provenance failure**.

{{illust:t-pillars|Scale in fog — size claims need provenience, not only awe.}}

## Three giant discourses (do not merge on first pass)

| Discourse | Home territory | Evidence style |
|---|---|---|
| **Biblical / Enochic giants** | Genesis, Numbers (Anakim etc.), 1 Enoch | Manuscripts, reception |
| **Folkloric / legendary tall peoples** | Regional oral histories, traveler tales | Ethnography; caution on colonial filters |
| **Newspaper / mound "giant skeleton" genre** | 19th–early 20th c. US press; later blogs | Clippings, often unverified; hoax literature exists |

Alternative media thrives when it **pastes** all three into one suppressed species. Method thrives when it **labels** which discourse a clip is using.

## What is actually solid

- Human height varies; **very tall individuals** exist in medical and archaeological records without requiring a separate antediluvian species.
- Many cultures tell stories of **powerful or oversized beings** — comparative mythology is real work.
- 19th-century American newspapers published numerous **giant skeleton** stories; historians and skeptics have documented patterns of **sensationalism, error, and fraud** alongside a smaller set of mundane tall burials misreported.

What is **not** solid until proven:

- a systematic Smithsonian "cover-up of giant bones" as demonstrated policy,
- a global Nephilim osteology collection hidden in basements,
- treating every mound-builder myth as identical to Genesis 6.

## How to evaluate a giant claim (checklist)

1. **Provenience** — exact site, excavator, date, repository?
2. **Primary record** — field notes, museum catalog number, peer-reviewed description?
3. **Measurement chain** — who measured the femur, with what method?
4. **Photo chain** — original plate vs AI-era collage vs re-staged prop?
5. **Alternative explanations** — misreported animal bone, pathological gigantism, hoax, unit conversion error?
6. **Text vs bone** — is the argument from Scripture/Enoch, or from osteology? Do not let one launder the other.

If a video offers only music + zoom on a clipping + "they don't want you to know," you have **rhetoric**, not a case file.

## Americas-focused claims in alternative media

Common motifs in Mr Mythos–adjacent and wider giant lore:

- mound sites as giant tombs,
- racialized "prior race" folklore (treat with extreme caution — colonial storytelling often invents or distorts bodies),
- west-coast and desert giant legends,
- linkage to Watcher bloodlines (lesson 10 stress-tests genetic rhetoric).

**EPISTEMIC_STANCE reminder:** single-villain ethnic stories and race-mysticism are treated as **weaker** than multi-actor institutional models. Giant lore has been used historically to dehumanize Indigenous peoples or to invent settler myths of prior civilizations. Observation includes **noticing that abuse**, not repeating it as entertainment.

## Old World parallels

Greek gigantomachy, Mesopotamian flood-era beings, and various "tall ancestor" motifs show that **oversized beings are a widespread mythic technology** for talking about power, chaos, and the time before law. Comparative work is legitimate. Comparative work that forces one DNA + one flood year + one museum conspiracy is collage.

## Observe, don't convert

You can take giant myths seriously as **culture** without promoting unverified osteology. You can investigate anomalous reports without treating every 1880s headline as FOIA gold.

> A giant in a story can move a people. A giant in a museum needs a catalog card.
