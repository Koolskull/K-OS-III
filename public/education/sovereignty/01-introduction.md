# What this track is for

This track is a set of short lessons for examining the sources of your beliefs, the historical record of institutional power, and a few primary spiritual and scientific reports that sit outside everyday media. It is not a manifesto and it is not a demand that you adopt any single conclusion.

K-OS already has an explicit rule for contested topics. Read [`docs/EPISTEMIC_STANCE.md`](../../../docs/EPISTEMIC_STANCE.md) if you have not. The short version: on topics where serious people disagree, the defaults present the mainstream account and the credible dissenting accounts, both sourced and attributed, and leave the conclusion to you. That rule applies here.

## What "deprogramming" means in this context

The word is used loosely in popular culture. Here it means something practical and limited:

- Notice where a belief came from (media, school, family, algorithm, personal experience, institutional statement).
- Separate what can be checked against primary documents from what is interpretation or narrative.
- Keep the ability to hold competing accounts without collapsing into certainty or into despair.

It is not a call to reject every institution, every government program, or every conventional historical account. Real institutional overreach has been documented. Ordinary human error, bureaucratic incentive, and incomplete information also exist. Both can be true at once.

## How the lessons are ordered

1. **Simple salvation teachings of Jesus** — a minimal, text-based spiritual baseline that does not require specialized knowledge.
2. **Robert Monroe and out-of-body experience** — one documented line of consciousness research and practice.
3. **MKUltra, Stargate, and declassified programs** — primary historical record of specific intelligence projects.
4. **Nikola Tesla and Walter Russell** — documented engineering and cosmology versus later popular claims.
5. **Jekyll Island, the Federal Reserve, and the American System tradition** — the 1910 meeting, competing interpretations of monetary power, and one living political-economy frame that treats the long-term contest as American System versus oligarchical financial power.

Each lesson starts with a short plain statement, then expands with sources. You can stop after the short version of any lesson.

## What this track is not

- It is not a claim that every problem in the world traces to one ethnicity, one religion, or one city.
- It is not a claim that Israel, Muslim-majority states, or any single actor is the sole driver of historical events. Overlapping institutional, financial, and ideological networks are more accurate as a working model than any single-villain story.
- It is not a requirement that you accept the American System frame or any particular conspiracy literature. Those are presented as one interpretive tradition among others.
- It is not medical, legal, or psychiatric advice. If you are dealing with distress, psychosis risk, or trauma, seek qualified help. Examining ideas is not a substitute for care.

## How to use it

Read the short version of a lesson first. If it is useful, open the primary sources listed. Compare them with whatever mainstream summary you already know. Keep notes of where the accounts agree and where they diverge. The point is the habit of checking, not the adoption of a new orthodoxy.
