## 2. The two polite positions, stated academically

1) Mainstream feminist account: legal/political reforms expanded women’s options; remaining inequities trace to structural and cultural constraints (e.g., Claudia Goldin, Understanding the Gender Gap, 1990; Career and Family, 2021). Domestic technology reallocated rather than erased labor (Ruth Schwartz Cowan, More Work for Mother, 1983).  
2) Mainstream conservative account: the “traditional family” worked; role complementarity is natural and good; departures increase harms (movement example: Phyllis Schlafly, The Power of the Positive Woman, 1977).

Historical correction (mainstream): Stephanie Coontz documents that the mid-century “traditional family” is a curated vignette, not a timeless norm (The Way We Never Were, 1992; Marriage, a History, 2005). Earlier records show composite economic/domestic labor by women across classes and regions (Alice Clark, 1919; Laurel Thatcher Ulrich, 1982; 1990; Olwen Hufton, 1995).

Contemporary dissenting critiques of “progress as net benefit”: Betsey Stevenson & Justin Wolfers (2009) document a decline in reported female happiness relative to men; subsequent mainstream responses debate the measure and its inference. Mary Harrington (Feminism Against Progress, 2023) and Louise Perry (The Case Against the Sexual Revolution, 2022) argue that some “freedoms” carried uncounted costs. Carrie Gress (The End of Woman, 2023) offers a theological critique. These are dissenting relative to the dominant progress narrative.

What the track does not claim: that one ethnicity or nation singularly explains outcomes; that theology alone settles empirical disputes; or that a curated 1950s photograph is the only alternative to current arrangements.

## 3. The adaptive-household thesis

Across eras and cultures, households reallocated labor with season, illness, travel, skill, and opportunity. Fixed scripts underperform adaptive fits. The track treats this as a descriptive historical finding, not an ideological demand.

Practice layer (door-agnostic): inventory actual abilities and needs (`role-inventory.md`), act on what is in front of you (`weekly-flow-log.md`), and notice what it becomes. The reader does not need to agree with a contested conclusion to use the tools.

## 4. Research notes on sex differences (signposts only)

Mainstream synthesis: Janet Shibley Hyde, “The Gender Similarities Hypothesis” (American Psychologist, 2005) — many measured psychological differences are small on average with substantial overlap; some domains show larger gaps.  
Polar positions: Simon Baron-Cohen, The Essential Difference (2003) argues for larger evolved differences in empathizing vs systemizing; Gina Rippon, The Gendered Brain (2019, popular science) cautions against neuroessentialist claims and measurement artifacts. The literature is contested; this track does not settle it.
