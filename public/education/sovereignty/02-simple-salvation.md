# Simple salvation teachings of Jesus

Before the archives and the towers and the island meetings, this track starts with a **small lamp**: the public-domain words of Jesus in the Gospels—not as a weapon against other lessons, but as a spiritual baseline that does not require a clearance, a subscription, or a secret decoder ring.

{{illust:narrow-path|A narrow path under a high light — motion along the road, not a billboard creed.}}

## Short version

Read Jesus’s own teaching in the Gospels (public-domain translations such as KJV, ASV, WEB):

- Love of God and neighbor as the weight of the law (Matthew 22:37–40).
- Mercy, forgiveness, and the refusal of performative religion (Matthew 6; Luke 15).
- The kingdom as present demand and future hope (Mark 1:15; Luke 17:20–21 in context of the whole Gospel).
- Salvation tied to faith, repentance, and following—not to trivia contests about secret history.

This lesson does **not** replace church, counseling, or careful theology. It is a text-first starting point.

## Expanded story

A person can spend years collecting scandals the way other people collect sports stats, and still never ask: *what kind of human am I becoming while I read this?* The Gospel scenes keep pulling the camera back to ordinary life—bread, debt, enemies, anxiety about tomorrow—while still speaking of eternity.

> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." (Matthew 11:28, KJV)

That line is not a FOIA document. It is a claim about the soul. Hold it in the same honest hand you use for declassified files: *what does the text say?* *what do traditions say it means?* *what do you do with it on a Tuesday?*

{{illust:simple-table|Bread and cup on a plain table — simple signs, slow pulse of light.}}

### Reading practice

1. Pick one Gospel (Mark is shortest). Read it through without commentary.
2. Note teachings of Jesus in his own voice vs. later framing in letters and creeds.
3. Only then open secondary theology—mainstream and dissenting—if you want depth.

Public-domain English options: **KJV**, **ASV**, **World English Bible**. The in-OS **HOLY BIBLE** app can sit beside this lesson.

### What this is not

- Not a demand that every reader share the project’s devotional framing.
- Not a license to ignore documented institutional crime because "faith covers it."
- Not a single-denomination catechism.

## Sources

- Public-domain Gospel texts (KJV / ASV / WEB).
- 1 Thessalonians 5:21 as the project’s epistemic motto.
- Your own community’s teachers, weighed against the text.

**How to use:** Read Mark or the Sermon on the Mount once slowly. Then continue the track with a calmer nervous system.
