## What this lesson is for

The Russian flowering: the fool as critic of power. Keep legend and record apart. Mark formal status.

Named positions:

- P1 Orthodox hagiographic: canonized fools confront rulers and protect the weak.
- P3 Academic-historical: the *yurodivy* as a performed role read by a community; legends grow.
- P5 Social/prophetic: the fool speaks frankly to power (parrhesia).

Status tags appear inline (HAGIOGRAPHY / CHRONICLE / DOCUMENTED / CONTESTED).

## Basil the Blessed (Vasily), Moscow (HAGIOGRAPHY + DOCUMENTED context)

- Dates and status: born Dec 1468; died **2 Aug 1557** (some say 1552); glorified **2 Aug 1588**. [OCA — VERIFIED]
- Cathedral: Ivan IV built the **Intercession (Pokrov) Cathedral** (1555–1561) for the capture of Kazan (not built by Basil). A chapel over Basil’s grave was added in 1588; the popular name followed. [OCA — VERIFIED]
- Gift story: the OCA records Basil **giving away** “a rich imperial present” to a destitute merchant; he also rebuked Ivan for daydreaming during Liturgy (HAGIOGRAPHY).

---

## Nicholas Salos of Pskov (HAGIOGRAPHY / CHRONICLE)

- Commemorated **28 Feb**; d. **1576**. In **Feb 1570**, after the Novgorod massacre, Nicholas confronted Ivan: “Ivanushko, eat our bread and salt, but not the blood of Christians,” and offered raw meat; when Ivan refused (Lent), Nicholas replied: “But you drink human blood.” Ivan left Pskov. [OCA; Pskov Chronicle cited — HAGIOGRAPHY/chronicle; treated carefully]
