## What this lesson is for

To replace fixed-role scripts with a documented pattern: households have always adapted roles to ability, health, travel, season, and opportunity.

> 🗣️ “Traditionally the aggregate of relationships fit based upon each others abilities and needs. Roles constantly shift in reality, sometimes people get sick, sometimes they heal, sometimes they travel, and one did homesteading depending on plans…”

This is widely compatible with mainstream social history: in farm, craft, and shop households, tasks moved with weather, markets, and illness. The curated vignette is the outlier in its rigidity.

---

## Examples from the record

- Early modern and colonial households: work moved between spouses and kin as seasons and cash flow changed (Ulrich, Good Wives, 1982; A Midwife’s Tale, 1990).
- Shopkeeping and cottage industry: women’s production and credit roles expanded or contracted with trade cycles (Clark, 1919).
- Industrial/office transitions: sector entry and exit traced to local wages, childcare, and educational timing (Goldin, 1990; 2021).

Labeling: adaptive roles are the mainstream historical pattern; prescriptive fixity is a modern cultural ideal more than a multi-century rule.
