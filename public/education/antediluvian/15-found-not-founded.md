# Found not founded — architecture claims

Across Jon Levi–style city walks, Sepehr-style megalith montages, and broader alternative archaeology, a recurring thesis appears: later cultures **found** monumental stone and plastered a founding myth over it — Romans at Baalbek, dynasties at Giza, republics in "old world" city halls. Lesson 03 introduced the "founded" problem at Göbekli and friends. This lesson generalizes the **claim type** and the **site-by-site method**.

{{illust:dig-grid|A grid over sacred ground — method as respect.}}

## The claim pattern

1. Stonework looks "too good" or "wrong style" for the official founders.
2. Tool-mark or fit precision is treated as out-of-place.
3. Local legend or esoteric tradition remembers older builders.
4. Conclusion: official history is a **rebrand** of recovered infrastructure.

Sometimes pieces of this pattern are fair questions (multi-phase sites are normal). The failure mode is **globalizing** every awkward joint into one antediluvian contractor.

## Multi-phase sites are normal

Archaeology expects:

- rebuilding,
- spoliation (reusing older blocks),
- prestige renovation,
- ideological re-founding by new elites.

"Found not founded" is sometimes **literally true** in the weak sense: people reuse places. The strong sense — **unknown antediluvian engineers as default** — needs positive evidence of that culture (tools, settlements, writing, dates), not only disbelief in the official name on the plaque.

## Method (steal this)

For any building claim:

1. **Phases** — draw a timeline of construction episodes.
2. **Inscriptions / archives** — what do texts contemporary to phases say?
3. **Quarries and logistics** — where did stone come from?
4. **Tool marks and experimental archaeology** — what has been reproduced?
5. **What would falsify** the alternative contractor hypothesis?

## Channel habits

- **Close-up masonry porn** without a phase plan is incomplete.
- **Music + drone** is not stratigraphy.
- **Comparing distant joints** is allowed; **equating dates** is not automatic.

## Observe, don't convert

Skepticism toward plaques is healthy. Skepticism toward plaques that becomes **universal suspicion without local work** is laziness. The best alternative researchers and the best mainstream archaeologists share one virtue: they get **specific**.

> "Found" can mean reused. It can also mean "I don't know yet." Only one of those is a full theory.
