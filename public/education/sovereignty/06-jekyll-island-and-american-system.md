# Jekyll Island, the Federal Reserve, and the American System tradition

November 1910. A private car. A cover story about duck hunting. An island off Georgia. Men who understand that the plumbing of money is the plumbing of power—and that the public will one day live inside whatever pipes they design.

{{illust:jekyll-night|Jekyll Island at night — water, trees, a lit house, a moon that does not take minutes.}}

## Short version

- The **Jekyll Island meeting** is historical fact: Senator Nelson Aldrich and leading bankers drafted a reserve-plan architecture in secret.
- That architecture fed the political fight culminating in the **Federal Reserve Act (1913)**. What the Fed *is* (a layered public-private central banking system) is documented; what it *means* morally and geopolitically is contested.
- G. Edward Griffin’s *The Creature from Jekyll Island* is a **named dissenting synthesis**—read beside the Act, Fed histories, and academic monetary history—not as the sole primary text.
- **American System** voices (historical Clay/Carey traditions; contemporary commentators such as Susan Kokinda and Barbara Boyd in the LaRouche-adjacent political-economy frame) treat long-run conflict as industrial republic vs oligarchical finance—including City of London networks—rather than a single ethnic villain. Present that as an **interpretive school**, not as mandatory OS doctrine.

## The island as stage play

They needed secrecy because panic still lived in living memory, and because democratic politics is noisy when banks write the quiet parts. Official and semi-official histories often describe Jekyll as a technical drafting retreat. Dissenting histories describe a coup in soft shoes. The meeting’s **existence** is not the same question as its **moral interpretation**.

### American System storytelling

In that tradition, the United States is not merely a market; it is a project: infrastructure, invention, sovereign credit, a population capable of high skill. Britain’s financial empire (and successors in global finance) appear as a rival operating system—not the only force on earth, but a recurring pressure. Kokinda and Boyd appear here as **talking heads inside that school**, useful to understand the school’s live rhetoric, not as infallible oracles.

### Conglomeration, not single villain

This track’s earlier promise stands: overlapping organizations, orders, and corporate entities can serve a small set of financial centers and secretive clubs **without** reducing history to one ethnicity or one foreign people as pure evil. Complexity is not cope; it is accuracy.
