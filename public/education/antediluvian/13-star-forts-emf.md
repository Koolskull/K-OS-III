# Star forts, domes, and EMF architecture

Star forts (trace italienne bastions), domed civic buildings, and ornate towers form a visual cluster in mud-flood / Tartaria media. Creators argue their geometry is optimized for **resonance, aether, or EMF distribution** — a planetary energy grid disguised as "military history" or "baroque taste." Military historians answer with **gunpowder geometry**. This lesson holds both chairs.

{{illust:set-geometry|Angles in plan — fortification math or energy glyph?}}

## Star forts: the mainstream engineering story

From the 15th–18th centuries, European fortification design responded to **cannon**:

- low thick walls resist artillery better than tall medieval curtains,
- angled bastions eliminate dead ground and enable flanking fire,
- geometry is textbook military engineering (Vauban and peers).

Primary-adjacent doors:

- period treatises on fortification,
- surviving plans in military archives,
- standard military-history surveys of siege warfare.

That story is detailed, boring in the best way, and predictive: star plans cluster where gunpowder states fought.

## The alternative energy reading

Tartaria-adjacent claims often hold that:

1. Star plans are **antenna / resonator** layouts.
2. Domes and cupolas are **collection or broadcast** structures.
3. Later "restoration" and demolitions disable the grid.
4. Textbooks rebrand energy architecture as forts and churches to hide function.

These claims usually rest on **formal resemblance** (sacred geometry language, radial plans) plus distrust of official function labels. Formal resemblance is a **hypothesis generator**, not a power-output measurement.

## Domes and "old world" ornament

Domes have structural and symbolic jobs: enclose large spaces, signal cosmos / authority, show engineering skill. Alternative media adds: wireless power, weather control, spiritual technology. Again — **possible as speculation**, unpaid as physics until someone shows:

- a working model,
- period technical literature describing the function claimed,
- or archaeological residue of the apparatus (not only pretty stone).

Wardenclyffe-style wireless power dreams (sovereignty track) sometimes get retroactively projected onto every dome. Projection is not proven continuity.

## Method checklist

1. **Plan first** — get the actual fort plan, not a game screenshot.
2. **Date construction phases** — many sites are multi-century palimpsests.
3. **Siege history** — was this fort attacked with cannon? Does the design match threats?
4. **Energy claim test** — what is generated, stored, or transmitted, in what units?
5. **Compare failures** — if it was a grid, what breaks when a fort is slighted?

## Observe, don't convert

You can admire bastion geometry without worshiping the War Department *or* the free-energy thumbnail. The productive tension is: **military history is genuinely sophisticated**; **alternative function claims must beat it on mechanism**, not only on mystery.

> An angle that stops a cannon is already a kind of power. Other powers need other proofs.
