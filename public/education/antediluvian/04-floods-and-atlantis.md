# Floods everywhere and Plato's date

Flood stories are **global**. Plato's Atlantis is **local to two dialogues**. Alternative history often glues them into one event. This lesson keeps the glue optional.

{{illust:flood-wave|A wave that is myth and mud at once — label which is which.}}

## Comparative flood myths (observe the pattern)

Cultures preserve deluge narratives: Mesopotamian (*Atrahasis*, *Gilgamesh* XI), Hebrew Genesis 6–9, Greek Deucalion, and many others outside the Mediterranean. Comparative mythology asks:

- shared deep memory of real floods?
- independent response to riverine life?
- literary diffusion?

Primary-ish doors:

- [Epic of Gilgamesh flood tablet tradition](https://www.britishmuseum.org/) (British Museum collections / translations)
- [Genesis 6–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%206-9&version=KJV) (public-domain KJV or your preferred translation)
- Local ethnographies for non-Near-Eastern flood cycles (use academic libraries)

Alternative media (Mr Mythos, John Levi, Sepehr-adjacent) often reads these as **one global catastrophe memory**. That is a **hypothesis about memory**, not automatically a global stratigraphic layer.

## Plato's Atlantis — what the text says

In *Timaeus* and *Critias*, Critias recounts a story allegedly from Solon via Egyptian priests: a powerful island empire beyond the Pillars of Heracles, war with prehistoric Athens, destruction by earthquakes and floods **in a day and night**, sunk into the sea.

Plato's internal chronology is often summarized as **about 9,000 years before Solon** (Solon ~early 6th century BCE) — which lands near **~9600 BCE** if taken literally. That number is why Atlantis talk collides with Younger Dryas and early Holocene sea-level rise.

Read the dialogues:

- [Plato, *Timaeus* (Perseus)](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Plat.+Tim.)
- [Plato, *Critias* (Perseus)](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Plat.+Criti.)

## Three reading habits for Atlantis

1. **Literary / philosophical** — Plato invents or reshapes a moral allegory (common classicist view).
2. **Euhemerist / historical kernel** — a distorted memory of a real culture (Thera, sea peoples, Ice Age coasts, etc.).
3. **Alternative high culture** — technologically advanced antediluvian civilization (YouTube-heavy frame).

Robert Sepehr and Atlantean Gardens-style content typically advance (3) with diffusionist anthropology. Academic classics typically lean (1), sometimes (2). **Label which frame a video is using.**

## Sea-level rise is real either way

Post-glacial **eustatic sea-level rise** drowned continental shelves worldwide. That is not fringe. What *is* fringe is equating every drowned shelf with Plato's moralized island empire.

| Fact / claim | Status habit |
|---|---|
| Holocene sea-level rise drowned coasts | Strongly evidenced |
| Flood myths exist cross-culturally | Strongly evidenced as literature |
| Myths = one global event at date X | Hypothesis; needs mechanism + date |
| Plato's numbers are literal history | Contested interpretive choice |
| Atlantis = specific modern site Y | Usually underdetermined |

## Observe, don't convert

When a video shows a flood myth, a meltwater pulse, and Plato's 9,000 years in one breath, pause:

1. Did they convert calendars explicitly?
2. Did they separate **text**, **geology**, and **excavation**?
3. What would falsify the single-event glue?

> Floods happened. Stories happened. Plato wrote. The rest is assembly — and assembly is where honesty lives or dies.
